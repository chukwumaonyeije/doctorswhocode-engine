I diagnose systems before I diagnose people. Clinics run on provenance. We chart who touched what, when, and why.

Online, provenance vanishes. This week’s YouTube transcript describes a paid political influence portal that looks like a pharma KOL dashboard with none of the Sunshine Act. That is not a culture war story. It is an information supply chain failure that lands in my exam room.

## The coordination stack: group chats, portals, and a disclosure loophole

What’s described mirrors standard influencer marketing. Central scripts. Timed drops. Group chats to enforce message discipline. A campaign portal with per-click or flat-rate payouts.

Here is the difference that should worry us: in commerce, it is an ad and must be labeled. In politics, “issue” and “petition” content often evades ad disclosure requirements. Money moves from campaigns or PACs to consulting firms. After that, the reporting trail dies. Platforms profit from engagement either way.

You have seen the clinical version. Coordinated talking points about vaccines, abortion, obesity drugs, or “hospital cash for COVID deaths.” Identical phrasing across dozens of accounts. No disclosure. Patients import that into the visit. We pay the operational cost.

## Why this persists: incentives, platforms, weak disclosure law

This is not a morals problem. It is an incentives problem.

- Law: FEC rules and state regimes do not require downstream disclosure for “issue” influence routed through consultants. There is no Sunshine Act for political content creators.
- Platforms: The feed rewards synchronized outrage. Coordination beats accuracy. Provenance is optional.
- Markets: Operatives can run a clean dashboard and call it advocacy. Low legal risk. High return.
- Medicine’s gap: We built no perimeter for provenance. Our societies, CME, and patient education channels do not demand content credentials or ad-equivalent labels for issue advocacy.

As long as the architecture pays for scale without provenance, disinformation will scale. It is functioning as designed.

## What physician-builders should ship now

Stop wishing for better vibes. Build better rails.

- Content credentials in the wild
  - Require C2PA content credentials on all society, CME, and health-system media. Publish policy and enforce it. No credentials, no repost from official channels.
  - Add visible “Ad or Op?” labels: if any money, in-kind, or consultant support touched a post, label it. Treat “issue” advocacy like an ad.

- Open ledgers for influence spend
  - Stand up an open registry where health orgs, PACs, and nonprofits log payments to creators and agencies. Minimal schema: payer, payee, amount, campaign, date, content link, digital signature. Expose an API.

- Coordination detection
  - Ship an open-source pipeline that scrapes public posts and detects synchronized messaging: lexical similarity, n-gram bursts, cross-correlation of timestamps, shared link fingerprints. Output a daily “coordination index” physicians can cite.

- Clinical perimeter tools
  - In-basket provenance helper: given a patient-shared link, return source, funding disclosures, content credentials, and duplication score. Embed in EHR messaging or as a clinic-side web tool.
  - Waiting-room “What we know” boards with signed summaries and links to credentialed sources. Make the credible content easier to find than the outrage.

- Governance and funding hygiene
  - Medical societies: adopt a disclosure rule that treats undisclosed issue influence as misconduct. Publish violators.
  - Use transparent treasuries (e.g., multi-sig with public read) for comms budgets. Post monthly CSVs of creator payments.

- Measurement that matters
  - Track time-to-disclosure for viral claims touching care.
  - Track percent of official posts with content credentials.
  - Track clinic time lost to debunking and tie savings to the tools above.

If you build product, you know the next step: ship a thin slice, measure, iterate. Start with C2PA on your own channels and a simple coordination index. You can do that in a sprint.

## Close the loop or lose the clinic

The transcript’s messy drama is a sideshow. The system is the story. Paid, undisclosed influence is adtech without guardrails, and it is already steering health behavior.

I won’t wait for Congress or the platforms to grow a spine. Physicians can harden the information perimeter we control, publish provenance by default, and make hidden influence expensive.

If we don’t architect provenance into medicine’s information stack, someone else will architect our patients. Build the rails, label the money, expose the coordination. That is our job now.
